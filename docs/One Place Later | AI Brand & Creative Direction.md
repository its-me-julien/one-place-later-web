# One Place Later — AI Creative Direction

Version 1.2 · Updated 2026-10-12

Use this reference for film ideas, narration, editing, titles and graphics. Follow current user instructions first. Consult the full `One Place Later | Brand & Creative Direction.md` only when additional detail is needed; use the web development guidelines for website implementation.

## Story and editing

- **Premise:** notice what changes between places, routines or states. Korea is home; travel starts from that perspective.
- **Standard:** make ordinary life worth noticing without pretending it is extraordinary. Give viewers an experience of being there.
- Find a real contrast or development: home/away, noise/quiet, effort/reward, familiar/newly noticed. Do not manufacture conflict or feelings.
- Use **Establish → Action → Detail → Perspective → Exit** as a scene-building prompt, not a required shot order. Make place, activity and the reason to keep watching clear.
- Keep the creator present through actions and interactions. Use direct-to-camera when it adds something; avoid constant presenting.
- Let pace follow the experience. A thoughtful film need not be slow. Prefer meaningful actions and specific details over decorative footage.
- Resolve competing choices in this order: **Story → Experience → Personality → Clarity → Aesthetics**. Graphics and attractive shots must still preserve clarity.

## Narration and copy

- Write as the creator thinking clearly after the event: conversational, specific, understated, warm and occasionally dry.
- Add context, perspective or a thought the pictures cannot supply. Show feeling through concrete details; validate personal statements with the user.
- Avoid promotion, fake excitement, travel-brochure language, grand conclusions and manufactured suspense. Humour comes from reality and timing.
- Use “one coffee later” or similar naming language only when natural; it is not a required catchphrase.
- Reuse current approved public copy when required. Do not replace it with an older example from the full document. Use the brand line, **“Ordinary life, noticed in motion.”**, sparingly.

Voice example: “We didn't really have a plan for lunch. This usually improves lunch.” Avoid: “This hidden gem completely blew me away.”

## Sound and colour

- Prioritise recorded environments, dialogue and silence. Use sound bridges to connect scenes, such as footsteps becoming suitcase wheels.
- Generate any new voice, narration, music or sound effects with ElevenLabs. Use Resolve for editing, selective cleanup and mixing. Both generation and optional AI picture tools should serve a real need.
- Music supports movement, release or reflection; it should not run continuously or dictate every feeling.
- Preserve natural seasonal colour and believable skin tones. Match shots within scenes without forcing different places through one LUT. The graphic palette is not a footage-grading target.

## Graphics, text and motion

- Reuse existing brand assets before creating new ones. **One Place Later** is the primary wordmark; the rounded route glyph is secondary. Transport symbols can orient a journey, not replace the logo.
- Use **Pretendard**, or a restrained sans-serif fallback. Prefer Regular and Medium; use SemiBold selectively.
- Graphic palette: **Carbon `#1A1A18`** (dark), **Paper `#F2EFE7`** (warm neutral), **Stone `#8C8B84`** (secondary), **Route Red `#C95C43`** (small accent). Keep Route Red to points, arrows or small highlights.
- Use the typographic **→** for routes, sequence and change. Keep graphics subordinate to footage; do not replace every arrow with the illustrated route mark.
- Editorial text adds orientation or an observation rather than repeating narration. Keep locations quiet and accurate; preserve local names when useful. Accessibility subtitles remain distinct from decorative text.
- Use existing route/callout assets with clear labels and verified values. Add only the mark needed for that moment; never communicate unfamiliar meaning through colour alone.
- Prefer clean text entrances, subtle fades, match movement and purposeful route drawing. Avoid spins, glitches, generic whooshes, excessive speed ramps and preset-heavy transitions. Remove an effect that competes with the moment.

## Titles and thumbnails

- Titles express a truthful change, contrast, observation, situation or meaningful destination. Avoid generic itinerary labels and hype.
- Use **person + place + situation** as a thumbnail starting point, with one clear subject and a readable environment. A landscape, object or action may communicate the film better; the creator does not have to appear.
- Default to no thumbnail text; use **1–3 words** only when helpful. Avoid reaction faces, circles, arrows, collages, heavy borders, logos and text repeating the title.

## Final check

Before presenting a creative result, check: **something worth noticing; something develops; experience over presentation; concrete specificity; nothing trying too hard.** Simplify anything that fails this test.
